# Applying the Mental Model: Firefly Jar

In the last section, you *read* a few JetLag games and tried to guess how they worked.
Now it's time to go the other direction and *build* one.
It's called Firefly Jar, and it's the game you played on the very first page of this book:

<iframe src="./firefly_jar_game.html"></iframe>

It's night in an overgrown garden.
Fireflies blink on at random spots, and blink out again a few seconds later.
Use the arrow keys to catch ten of them, then get to the jar before the sun comes up.
Every so often, a golden firefly appears in one of the garden's hard-to-reach corners.
It's worth three, but it doesn't stay lit for long.

Play it a few times before you read any further.
Try to win, and try to lose, too.
Notice how the sky slowly turns pink as dawn gets closer, and how a firefly flickers right before it goes out.
Those little touches are a big part of why the game feels more substantial than the four prototypes in the previous section.

Firefly Jar is small, but it's a complete game.
It has a goal, a clock, a way to win, a way to lose, and a way to play again.
By the end of this section, you'll have built all of it, and you'll understand every line.

We're going to build it the way real games get built.
First comes a version made entirely of plain gray shapes, because solid-color shapes are quick to make and easy to change.
Once the gray version is fun, and only then, will it get more style.
So this section has three parts.
In Part 1, the garden becomes a place you can walk around.
In Part 2, it becomes a game.
In Part 3, it gets dressed up.
